Valentino: Fashion as a Field of Interferences

In a setting defined by architectural tension, Alessandro Michele reflects on fashion’s own push and pull.

At Palazzo Barberini, grandeur has transformed over the centuries. For Fall/Winter 2026, Valentino leaned into that instability, staging a collection that treated fashion as a live negotiation between order and impulse.

The Baroque palazzo, with its measured façade and aged interiors, set the tone. It is a building shaped as much by friction as by form, where the disciplined clarity of artist Gian Lorenzo Bernini meets the disorienting curves of architect Francesco Borromini, inspiring artistic director Alessandro Michele, per his show notes. That same push and pull played out on the runway, where garments oscillated between structure and release, control and abandon.

And yet, for all its intellectual undercurrent, the collection never felt heavy-handed or compromised on aesthetic indulgence—it is Alessandro Michele, after all. Instead, it carried a lightness of spirit, as well as a willingness to let contradiction exist without over-explanation. In this season, Valentino proposed fashion not as a fixed statement, but as a field of interferences: a place where discipline and desire meet, collide, and, crucially, coexist.
