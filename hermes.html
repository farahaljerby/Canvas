Hermès: Twilight, Romance, Utility

At Hermès, the ephemeral hours between day and night set the mood. Shaped by the shifting colours of dusk, Nadège Vanhee explores what she calls a “liminal realm” for fall/winter 2026. A space between two states, reflected in a runway that moves from the last vestiges of a warmer afternoon, into the deeper, muted tones of a quiet, cooler evening. Presented at the La Garde Républicaine, the final runway saw 63 looks—each one offering familiarity for someone accustomed to the Hermès universe, yet seen through a more atmospheric lens.

That interplay between structure and ease continues in the tailoring. Long equestrian coats and defined shoulders sit alongside zipped A-line skirts, fitted dresses and brushed-denim suits. Dressage-collar shirts, knitted silk ties and two-tone cashmere bring the collection back to the language of riding, without making it feel like a literal exercise in equestrian dressing. The result is a wardrobe that feels rooted in the house’s codes, but not confined by them.

Print is used sparingly, with A.M. Cassandre’s Perspective motif serving as the collection’s sole graphic. Its appearance across select silk shirts, outerwear and dresses offers a considered contrast to the predominantly solid-coloured looks.
